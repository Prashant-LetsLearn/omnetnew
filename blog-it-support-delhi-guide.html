7 Questions to Ask Every Delhi IT Company Before Signing

1. What exactly is your SLA — in writing?

A real SLA specifies response and resolution times per priority level. For example: P1 (business down) — 20 minute remote response, 2 hour onsite. P2 (major degradation) — 1 hour remote, 4 hour onsite. If an IT company in Delhi cannot give you a written SLA with specific times, walk away. 'We will come as soon as possible' is not an SLA.

2. Who specifically will support my account — and what is their backup?

Many small Delhi IT vendors assign you to a single engineer. When that person is sick, on leave, or quits, you have no support. Ask specifically: who is my account manager, who are the backup engineers, and what is the escalation path. A proper MSP has L1, L2, and L3 coverage so no single person is a bottleneck.

3. Are you CERT-In empanelled?

CERT-In empanelment is a government-recognized credential for cybersecurity capability. Not every IT company needs it, but if your Delhi business handles client data, is in a regulated sector, or requires security audits — only a CERT-In empanelled company can produce valid audit reports. OMNET IT Solutions is CERT-In empanelled, one of relatively few Delhi MSPs with this credential.

4. What does your contract cover — and what is explicitly excluded?

Cheap IT contracts in Delhi often exclude the things that matter most: hardware parts, out-of-hours support, project work, third-party licensing issues. Read the exclusions list as carefully as the inclusions. A Rs 5,000/month AMC that excludes everything meaningful is worth less than a Rs 15,000/month contract that covers your real risks.

5. Can I speak to three current clients in Delhi?

Any reputable IT company in Delhi should be able to provide three references you can call. If they hesitate or give you contacts who turn out to be vague, that tells you everything. Ask the references specifically: how fast do they actually respond, and have they ever missed SLA?

6. How do you handle cybersecurity — not just IT support?

IT support and cybersecurity are no longer separable. An IT company that only handles helpdesk without monitoring for threats is leaving you exposed. Ask what they do for endpoint protection, email security, and patch management as part of the standard service. If cybersecurity is an optional add-on that costs extra, reconsider.

7. What is the exit clause?

Being locked into a bad IT contract in Delhi is painful. Ensure the contract has: a reasonable notice period (30-60 days), a full documentation handover clause (passwords, network diagrams, asset list), and no penalties for early exit after the minimum term. OMNET's contracts include all of these as standard.

Delhi-Specific IT Challenges to Be Aware Of

  • Power quality: Delhi offices face frequent voltage fluctuations. Good IT support includes UPS monitoring and power protection as part of infrastructure management.
  • ISP diversity: Single-ISP dependency is a significant risk in Delhi. A good IT partner will recommend dual-ISP failover for business-critical connectivity.
  • Physical security at Nehru Place: Delhi's largest IT market, Nehru Place, has many unverified vendors. Always verify company registration and physical office before signing an AMC.
  • Traffic and SLA: Delhi traffic means 'onsite within 2 hours' from a Nehru Place vendor can realistically mean 4 hours to Aerocity or Dwarka. Clarify which areas get which onsite SLA.
